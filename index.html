Recommendation to the CEO of IE University

Protect the premium. Test the unbundled future.

AI makes learning more accessible, and employers and platforms increasingly build and recognize skills outside universities. Yet premium degrees may keep their value as signals of selection, judgment, networks and career access. IE should bet neither on the death nor on the permanence of degrees.

What we ask you to decide today

  • Approve a conditional €2M, 12-month innovation portfolio ceiling.
  • Release €7,500 for a 4-week discovery; appoint an academic sponsor and a venture lead.
  • Allow a €52,500 pilot only if an employer pays first.
  • Not today: a credentialing platform, or the full €300,000 H3 envelope.

What we know

  • WEF 2025: employers expect 39% of workers' core skills to change by 2030.
  • OECD 2026: skills-first hiring is growing, but degrees and experience still both matter.
  • Firms that dropped degree requirements changed fewer than 1 in 700 hires (Harvard and Burning Glass).

What IE already has

  • ChatGPT Edu across the academic ecosystem since February 2025.
  • IEU Labs real projects; Headspring corporate academies.
  • Alumni lifelong learning, certificates and micro-credentials.

So "another AI tutor" is not a new strategy for IE.

Signals we watch

  • Employer hiring practices: are degree requirements really dropping?
  • Adoption of independently assessed credentials.
  • Demand for premium programs.
  • Corporate learning spend.

Strategic challenge

How might IE defend the distinctive value of premium education while selectively capturing growth in a world where learning and recognition become more distributed?

Five initiatives, each something new for IE. Compare our split with the classic 70/20/10 to see what changes.

How we run it

  • H1, Enabler: academic schools own the redesign, with dedicated support.
  • H2 and H3, Producer features: a small venture team with a named owner and a protected exploratory budget.
  • Innovation Growth Board (academic, finance, commercial, legal) decides at every gate: discover, pilot, then scale, pivot or stop.

M3

One experiment: IE Open Credentials

The sharpest question in the portfolio: will an employer pay IE to assess capability its people built outside IE? We test it with money, in stages, each with a stop rule.

First buyer

Head of L&D or Talent in a large employer

Already training middle managers in AI and business problem-solving. Needs to know who is ready for development opportunities or complex assignments. A hypothesis, not validated research.

The product

Applied AI & Business Judgment assessment

2 or 3 realistic business cases, uncertain evidence, AI allowed under stated conditions, a recommendation defended to qualified human assessors using IE rubrics.

The output

An evidence report per person

Not a diploma, not alumni status, not ECTS credits, not an implied MBA substitute. Formal credentials need later academic and legal approval.

Brand guardrail

The pilot report is never marketed as a degree-equivalent IE qualification: no alumni rights, no ECTS credits, no implied MBA substitute. We monitor reputation and cannibalization risk. Regulatory feasibility of later formal credentials is an open condition.

M4

The partner: buy execution, not trust

IE can design the standard but lacks an auditable workflow to run external assessments. We use a specialist for operations and keep standards, decisions and brand inside IE.

Build, partner, buy or invest?

Partner our choiceWorking tools now; the standard stays ours.
BuildSlow and costly for a test that may stop after €7,500.
BuyOut of proportion for an unvalidated need.
InvestA stake gives learning, not a working tool for the pilot.

The deal

A paid venture-client pilot with Vervoe, provisional on technical and legal diligence. Up to €25,000, inside the M3 pilot's €52,500 and only after an employer has paid. Not an acquisition, not equity.

  • IE owns the framework, rubrics, decisions and learner experience
  • Partner delivers tests, analytics and an audit trail
  • If diligence fails, we test the runner-up

The partner cannot prove employers value the report. M3's buyer-use test does.

M5

The culture: entrepreneurial speed with academic rigor

A new business needs fast, falsifiable commercial tests. A university cannot compromise assessment integrity, academic authority, student fairness or its premium brand. Both must hold.

What we observe

Established approval and academic-quality processes for formal education; public AI experimentation and corporate collaboration.

What we assume, not observe

That approval cycles, faculty incentives or department boundaries would slow this venture. We do not call IE bureaucratic without internal evidence; staff interviews should confirm or reject it.

Test commercial assumptions quickly; never shortcut academic standards. Reward useful learning and responsible stopping, not only launches or certificates issued.
